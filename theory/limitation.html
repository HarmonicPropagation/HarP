Limitations¶

The HarP procedure relies on several approximations that are listed below.

Physical Constraints¶

  1. The driving laser field is assumed to be linearly polarised.

  2. The interaction is restricted to cylindrical geometry.

  3. The polarisation response is assumed to be induced solely by the local fundamental field. With current laser technology, the harmonic energy is several orders of magnitude lower than that of the fundamental field. Additionally, for a long laser pulse with respect to its oscillation period, only the local field intensity should be responsible for the non-linear polarisation.

  4. The ionic susceptibility is neglected with respect to the electronic and atomic susceptibility.

  5. The propagation equations are solved within the paraxial approximation.

  6. The temporal envelope is assumed to vary slowly compared to the laser oscillation period. Consequently, the laser duration \(\mathbf{T_{fwhm}}\) must be significantly longer than the reference optical period \(\mathbf{T_r = 1/\omega}\).

  7. Although users can provide their own tables for the microscopic response, the tables currently distributed with HarP are obtained by solving the 3D time-dependent Schrödinger equation within the single active electron approximation, following the methodology of [KulanderShore1990].

  8. The target density must be much lower than the reference density \(\mathbf{\rho_r}\). This condition ensures laser propagation through the target (no reflection), and guarantees the validity of the refraction index perturbation approximation \(\chi \ll 1\).

  9. Within the single active electron approximation, only the first ionisation is considered significant.

  10. The atomic form factors are provided by the user. While their values are well established for high-energy photons [Henke1993], [NIST], significant discrepancies may exist for low-order harmonics depending on the approximations used in the calculation. Consequently, variations of the absorption cross-section may give rise to differences in harmonics properties.

Numerical Constraints¶

  1. The dipole polarisabilities are interpolated from precomputed tables. These tables should provide high-resolution dipole moments as a function of laser intensity and harmonic order for a given set of wavelengths and atomic species.

  2. Only two tables are currently available: argon and neon at \(\mathbf{810}\) nm. Extension to other wavelengths associated with conventional laser gain media are in progress.

  3. The spatial and temporal grids are uniformly discretised. While most harmonics are generated near \(r=0\) in most cases, an adaptive grid could be appropriate to reduce the radial discretisation resolution. Additionally, for far-field projection using a Hankel transform, a non-uniform grid based on the zeros of the Bessel function would provide better resolution.

  4. The computational complexity is \(\mathbf{O((N_q+1) \times N_t \times N_z \times N_r)}\). The estimated execution time for a full simulation is \(6 \times (N_q+1) \times (N_r/512) \times (N_z/256) \times (N_t/256)\) seconds, measured on an Apple MacBook Air M5 (16 GB RAM).
